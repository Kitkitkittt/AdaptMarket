# Roadmap

## 1. Expand the market-research corpus

Broaden coverage across:

- macroeconomics and monetary policy;
- industry supply and demand;
- commodities and input costs;
- interest rates, currencies, and credit conditions;
- government policy and regulation;
- sector outlooks and thematic research;
- company earnings, guidance, and valuation;
- timestamped financial and business news.

Research material provides market structure, historical theses, sector frameworks, and company sensitivities. News adds evidence that can confirm, weaken, or invalidate those views.

## 2. Connect company research into a market knowledge network

Company reports contain fragmented information about customers, suppliers, competitors, cost exposures, pricing power, market share, and capacity. The target representation connects:

```text
Company → Business segment → Product → Commodity → Supplier → Customer → Competitor → Industry → Macro factor
```

This enables evidence learned about one company to inform analysis of related companies and sectors.

## 3. Model cross-sector spillovers

The model should assess direct, second-order, and third-order effects rather than treating every market event as universally positive or negative.

Example: an oil-price increase may lift upstream producer revenue while raising petrochemical feedstock costs, increasing synthetic-rubber and tire-production costs, pressuring transportation margins, and affecting inflation expectations and interest-rate conditions.

The effect depends on a company’s value-chain position, contract structure, inventory cycle, pricing power, hedging policy, and ability to pass costs to customers.

## 4. Build thesis-conditioned event-impact analysis

New evidence should be assessed against an existing research context, including:

- historical investment theses;
- revenue and cost sensitivities;
- earnings forecasts;
- management guidance;
- valuation assumptions;
- prior catalysts and risks;
- peer and industry developments.

The intended output separately evaluates fundamental, thesis, valuation, market, and spillover impact, plus evidence confidence and material uncertainty.

## 5. Create a point-in-time market evidence graph

Research conclusions should link to timestamped evidence so the system can reconstruct what was known at a specific date. The graph will connect research claims, source documents, company and sector exposures, forecast assumptions, market events, supporting or conflicting evidence, and subsequent thesis revisions.

## Long-term vision

The long-term objective is a thesis-conditioned market-intelligence engine that turns research and incoming news into company-specific and cross-sector impact signals. The system should answer what changed, which companies are exposed, how the effect propagates, which theses are affected, and whether evidence is already reflected in expectations or valuation.
